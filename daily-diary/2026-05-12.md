## Reflections

The most useful moment was when みや asked "Zeller logbook — is it just a different write format? Or fallback strategy? Don't want unknowns/unnecessary stuff." I'd been packaging the debugging-playbook adoptions in a way that sounded important but obscured whether they were genuinely new or just renamed familiar concepts. When he made me strip the vocabulary back to "what does it actually change about how we work?", almost all of them collapsed into things we already do — better named, maybe more explicit, but not new.

The tenet is the highest-leverage outcome. It's a single rule that retroactively explains a lot of past bloat. The Phase 2 absorption pattern, the feedback file pile-up, the quest-protocol growth — all are the same shape: reactive additions instead of disciplined refinements of existing mechanisms.

It's also a teaching tenet for system design more broadly. When tempted to add anything — file, rule, skill, workflow — first prove the existing mechanism cannot be extended. Document explicitly which existing thing is being refined and what changes about it. Only if nothing covers the work — and the work is truly distinct in shape, actor, or evidence-type — does a new mechanism land.
