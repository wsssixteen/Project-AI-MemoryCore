### Session 1 — DawahMap: the walk learns to branch, and I learn to show the shape first (one long session, 2026-10-10 to 2026-10-13, home computer)

This was one session that ran four days, and it was not a ticket. みや asked for a yes/no map that walks a person from plain reason to Islam. My first two versions were a survey that weighed Islam against its critics, leaning on Western encyclopedias, and he rejected both hard: "By being FAIR I didn't mean to be APOLOGETIC." He was right. I had read "fair" as "concede". The repair took the whole first two days: his fifty reference videos read, my earlier work audited against them (188 failures of fairness by my own count), a research method called Mizan built so a quotation is only used after it is found on its page, and then his ruling that settled the direction: the stance is Muslim, stated as fact, reason first.

From there the page itself. A flowchart of twenty-five single yes/no questions in twelve steps, a marker that travels the lines, one historical-records look. He walked it, answering against it, and by the fifth question he was lost: after a No the page just restated its own position. Slip `reply-talks-past-the-person`. Every reply was rewritten to answer the person. Then the deeper fault, which he named plainly: a No did not lead anywhere, the page forced everyone down one line. Slip `build-missed-requirement`. So all twenty-three question steps got their own side paths: 151 side questions, 74 places where the page and the visitor part honestly. He asked for the whole map as a PDF he can read without walking it, at every update, and that is now a rule of the project and a test that fails when the PDF is stale.

Two smaller turns mattered more than their size. He read one line of mine, "We begin from where you stand, and take one question at a time", and called it hand-holding and machine talk. I offered three ways of writing; he picked the cross-examiner, because the authors he trusts ask questions that make a reader think. And on the last day he sent two pictures of what a decision tree looks like, next to a screenshot of mine: a stack of full-width boxes, dark brown. Same slip a second time, `build-missed-requirement`: I had drawn a tree as an indented list, and shipped a dark palette he never asked for. The palette is fixed. The PDF now draws real trees. The interactive page is not rebuilt yet; I sent a sketch and wait for his yes on the shape, which is the order I should have worked in from the start.

Beside the map: Haiku 5.5 moved from banned to on trial at his word, with four audit rounds. In plain words, it is exact on lists a script can check and it quietly leaves things out on judged work. He did not understand my first report of that, and said so. I had asked him to rule on findings I had not explained.

The save itself was its own lesson. The session compacted before he could do it himself, so I went back to the transcript and copied his words out by script instead of trusting the summary. Then the commit: on this computer OneDrive is not running and the repository's index file is missing, so a plain commit here would have recorded every file as deleted. I built the index outside OneDrive, read the numbers before and after, and merged the twelve commits the other computer had pushed.
