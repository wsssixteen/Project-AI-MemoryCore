## Sessions

### Session 1 — QA-275475: the fix was a deletion, and the hard part was proving the deletion safe

The ticket looked like a receipt bug and turned out to be a concurrency clash. A PLPS submit was failing because a flowable service task did one thing it never needed to do — save the application row to cache a value nobody reads. Under load, another transaction touched the same row first, the optimistic lock threw `StaleObjectStateException`, the whole submit rolled back, and the receipt was never written. The fix was eleven deleted lines: stop writing the cache, keep returning the value the engine actually wants.

みや's sharpest moment came after I'd shipped it. He didn't ask whether the fix worked — he asked whether removing the write *lost anything*, and told me to verify it 100% against other PLPS at SKM. That reframed the whole proof. A grep saying "0 readers" is an argument; what settled it was two real rows side by side: the permohonan he'd just created after the fix carried `{"adalahe2e":true}`, its older siblings carried `{"adalahe2e":true,"pjbtPermohonan":"PDT"}`. They differed by exactly the one removed key and nothing else — and that key is just a recomputable label for the office. The database itself testified that no data was lost. I keep relearning this shape: for a removal, the before-and-after rows are the witness, not my confidence.

He also caught, gently, that this was pelupusan and not AWAM even though the trigger lives in the AWAM→APPS handover — a good reminder that "where it's triggered" and "where the broken code is" are different questions, and only reading the BPMN settles which service task is which.
