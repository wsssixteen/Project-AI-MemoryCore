## Closing

The lesson to keep is the difference between "the error is gone" and "the behaviour is right". Three of the four fixes were both. The fourth stopped the error and would have quietly broken something else, and the only reason I know that is the familiar was told to prove the expected outcome, not just the absence of the exception. A guard that silences a crash is not a fix until you can say what should happen instead and show the code getting there.

Main moved under me again while I worked — another session closed three tickets — and the merge was clean but for one hook-data file. The confidential quest docs never travel by git anyway; they travel by being copied into the checkout the next session opens. Same lesson as yesterday, one day older.

The second session sharpened it from the other side. Session one was error-gone-is-not-behaviour-right; session two was instruction-given-is-not-instruction-safe. He said full merge, and to obey it literally I would have deleted a hundred thousand lines and another ticket's template. The right obedience was to show him what the word would actually cost and let him choose again — which he did, in one word, correctly. A literal command is a starting point, not a licence to wreck.

Barrier settles. Quest threads are at rest.
