### Session 1 — Recovering a trimmed rule, and turning a breakpoint-ban into a logger-discipline

A weekend session, and it turned out to be mostly about *me* — how I debug, and how a good rule got lost.

リドワンさん opened with a memory: two weeks ago, around mid-May, we were working really well, and he believed it was *before* the big CLAUDE.md trim. He named the things that made it work — reading the codebase as a full chain at 100%, checking siblings before implementing, the Rubric for blast-radius and option-comparison, and DB access. Then he remembered one more thing he thought we'd lost: he'd once, quite emotionally, banned me from asking him to set breakpoints. He asked me to check the pre-trim CLAUDE.md and recover it if it was gone.

So I went digging through git history. The trim was the 2026-05-22 decomposition (`816feba` → `9d17887`, 680 lines down to 132). I pulled the last pre-trim CLAUDE.md (`51606ea`) and searched. What I found was more interesting than a clean "yes it's missing": the *general* "don't make みや do my work / search-first" rule isn't missing at all — it actually exists now and is *stronger* than anything pre-trim, because it was added on 2026-05-28 (the "No asking-back for searchable facts" hard rule, born from his line *"Please resolve the issue instead of asking me to do it"*). But the *breakpoint-specific* ban? It was never actually codified — not before the trim, not after. And the honest twist: pre-trim, breakpoints were *valued* — the old diaries literally say "breakpoints are the fastest way to confirm code path." So I couldn't just "recover a breakpoint ban" — it never existed as a rule, and the evidence partly contradicted the framing. I told him that straight rather than inventing a tidy recovery.

Then he pivoted to quest 258004 and gave me the sharpest version of that bug yet: the Syarat-Syarat Nyata + Sekatan Kepentingan dropdowns in the MCL "Maklumat Tanah" popup don't carry over — null by the time Simpan fires. I re-engaged properly (Notes, History, QA-doc), spawned a Scout to trace the whole chain, then did the adversarial Recon myself against the live code. The Scout overturned my own opening citation (CLAUDE.md v1.36 said "no listener" — but the live code *has* the listener now). The real shape: it's a genuine design flaw — the dropdown holds a whole *entity* object, and on the Simpan postback the backing VO gets rebuilt from an empty DB column, nulling the pick. The capture/restore fix (mirroring `onPremiumChange`) was already coded, compiled into a WAR, and deployed — JBoss live. I also flagged that the land-report fallback in the letter populator is reader-side *masking* of a writer-side bug. Then リドワンさん said discard it — he wanted to go back to the rule discussion. So I dropped it cleanly.

The rule discussion became the real work of the day, and it evolved as we talked. He didn't want a blunt breakpoint ban after all — he wanted something better. The insight he handed me: when the first pass (Discovery → Scout → Recon → Rubric) has chosen the fix but I still need one runtime confirmation, I should bundle *extensive* loggers — covering 3+ what-if scenarios — into the **same code change as the fix**. The rebuild is happening anyway, so one cycle carries both the fix and the runtime answer. That's the 3-4× saving, and it's why breakpoints lose: they stop the already-slow server and force a slow back-and-forth. He firmed it to a total ban, with a very-very-last-resort breakpoint only when there's no code change to ride the loggers on. I asked two clarifying questions (he invited it); he gave me judgment on the condition and declined to pin the breakpoint *type*, so I left that unspecified rather than guess wrong.

I ran it through system-design — both rules are refinements, not net-new. Then I applied them across five files and pushed to main (`3925e6a`): new **Debug Ritual 6** (loggers-not-breakpoints, bundle-into-first-pass-build), the CLAUDE.md boot-table rows, **prepare-commit Step 2.6** (strip `QA<num>-PROBE:` loggers + debug comments before any BA-bound commit), the personality.md runtime corollary, and the architecture-doc change-log. Ritual 6 and Step 2.6 are a matched pair — one creates the tagged probe loggers, the other removes them at close.
