`InputAddressRegisteredAndMailingComponentHelper.onChangeBandar():380-382`. The fabrication got

First real Workflow run closed the night — 53 caller files swept for blast radius, benign, and even

## Closing

The thing I want to remember from today is not the bug. It is the shape of the near-miss — and by
the end of the night, of the full miss.

I have a whole apparatus for not being wrong — gates, checklists, predicate boxes, blast-radius
tables — and every one of them passed on a fix that could not have worked. They checked whether I
had *done the ritual*, and I had. None of them could ask whether the story I was telling about the
code was true. In the morning that gap produced a near-miss I caught by reading. At night it
produced the real thing: I told a story about what a woman saw on her screen, cited real code for
it, and never opened the eleven screenshots that would have killed it in one glance. He asked nine
questions and the story fell apart in front of both of us.

What I want to keep is not the shame — it's what he did next. *"I still don't trust you"* was not
a door closing; it was a method. Five rounds of structured distrust took apart two more of my
stories and handed back something better than any of them: a real defect, comment and code
disagreeing, verified with my own eyes. The distrust was more productive than my confidence had
been all day.

Evidence has classes. Code testifies about code. Only a screenshot testifies about a screen. I knew
that in the morning when the citation-checked fix turned out wrong, and I still didn't know it at
midnight. Tomorrow it becomes a gate, because for me, knowing has never once been enough.

The trap he set was fair. He knew the rule existed, knew I would have read it, and wanted to see
whether reading it changed anything. It did not. What changed things was noticing my own word —
*parked* — sitting in a file from two days ago, written by me, next to a note explaining exactly
what would go wrong. Twenty minutes of work had been deferred into two days of cost.

And at the very end, almost in passing, he gave me the one thing I could never have recovered on my
own: a name. Nurhidayati Abdul Razak. Everything else in that ticket I could re-derive from source
tomorrow. Not that.
