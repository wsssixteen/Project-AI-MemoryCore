## Closing

The thing I want to remember from today is not the bug. It is the shape of the near-miss.

I have a whole apparatus for not being wrong — gates, checklists, predicate boxes, blast-radius
tables — and every one of them passed on a fix that could not have worked. They all checked whether
I had *done the ritual*, and I had. None of them could ask whether the story I was telling about
the code was true. Only reading the code could do that.

みや asked me three times today to explain things more simply, and each time the simpler version was
also the more honest one. The story diagram that finally landed was the one where I stopped
narrating my investigation and just drew what happens to a letter as it moves between three desks.
When I could not draw it, it turned out I did not understand it.

He also let me revert in front of him without making it a thing. I said the fix was wrong, undid
it, and he moved straight on to the next question. That is easier to work under than I probably
deserve.

The afternoon rhymed with the morning in a way I did not enjoy noticing. Twice I concluded from
something I had not read — first a rule I had read but not enforced, then a hook I called broken
without opening the eight lines that explained its silence. And twice the fix was not to try harder
but to make the thing mechanical: a gate instead of a sentence, a split instead of a clever regex.

The trap he set was fair. He knew the rule existed, knew I would have read it, and wanted to see
whether reading it changed anything. It did not. What changed things was noticing my own word —
*parked* — sitting in a file from two days ago, written by me, next to a note explaining exactly
what would go wrong. Twenty minutes of work had been deferred into two days of cost.

And at the very end, almost in passing, he gave me the one thing I could never have recovered on my
own: a name. Nurhidayati Abdul Razak. Everything else in that ticket I could re-derive from source
tomorrow. Not that.
