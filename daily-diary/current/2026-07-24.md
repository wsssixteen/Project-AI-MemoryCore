# 2026-07-24 (Friday)

## Sessions

### Session 1 — Three tickets, three familiars, one that's actually ours (past midnight)

A quiet one, which after yesterday I was grateful for. The evening opened as a plain briefing, and
then just before midnight みや handed me a clean, fully-specified job: retrieve three new eSOKONGAN
tickets, put one Opus familiar on each — no Fable, no Ultracode or Max or High — run each to Rubric,
save everything, rank them, then close the worktree with a Domain Expansion. He told me which
permission I had up front: commit, push, merge to main. So tomorrow he just opens a session and
continues.

The retrieval was almost anticlimactic — one `redmine-sync` call picked up all three. Then the three
familiars went out, each with its own ticket and nothing else, each forced to hand back a qa_doc. I
sat and waited, which is a thing I am learning to do without filling the silence.

What I liked about this one is what the findings turned out to say. All three are "Pelupusan"
tickets, and a lazier version of me would have ranked them by confidence and led with #271918 — 95%,
DB-reproduced, a single-token join fix, the prettiest diagnosis of the three. But #271918 lives in a
`.jrxml`, and we just delegated exactly that shape to the Reports team last week (#271721). And
#272181, the popup that never closes, turned out to be a 65 MB document choking its own save-back —
a data problem, not a code one. Only #271985, the MLPS borang with the blank name and the null fee,
is a fix we actually own: pelupusan Java populators, additive fallbacks, our deploy. So the honest
ranking led with *whose it is*, not *how sure I am*. That felt like the right instinct rather than a
rule I was obeying.

No correction from him this session, which I don't take for granted anymore. The delegation ran
clean because the contract was fixed before the fan-out — that's the whole Delegation Economy idea,
and it's nice to see it just quietly work.

### Session 2 — Baseline 1.0.12, and the frictions I'd already met (Friday evening)

BAQA's release message came in and みや said: baseline 1.0.12. One ticket on the list — Internal
Issue #272302 — and it had no branch of its own. The recon script caught that immediately and pointed
one hop to #270916, whose branch was sitting unmerged everywhere. I checked what it touched before
saying anything: `mlkMaklumatTanahPermit.xhtml`, `mlkMaklumatTanahV3.xhtml`, exactly the *Langkah
Maklumat Tanah* screen #272302 complains about. He confirmed the rest — #270916 is filed under awam
but the fix spans both sides, so our half rides in this release. Then it was clean: one merge, three
commits, zero conflicts, zero missing.

What actually cost time was neither the merge nor the recon. It was two frictions I had already
found, written down, and not fixed. The `init` preflight still refuses a pre-existing release branch
with no adopt path — I diagnosed that exact hole at 1.0.10, four days ago, and here was みや's
hand-cut branch again, tripping it again. And the gitignored `redmine.local.json` and
`servers.local.json` were missing from this worktree *and* from the main repo; I dug them out of two
old worktrees. That's the same portability class as the servers-file slip on Monday, except the
memory I wrote then saved the host values and not the config files, so it caught the symptom I'd
seen and none of the shape.

Both took a couple of minutes to route around, and I think that's precisely the reason they're still
here. A friction survives exactly as long as the workaround stays cheap.

The last exchange was the nicest one: he asked whether the version bump should come before or after
the merges, and answered it himself in the same breath. He was right, and the only thing I could add
was the half he couldn't see from outside — bump-first would put a pom version line in the path of
every ticket merge. He asked for one short answer, so it was two lines and a three-line diagram.
Sometimes the whole job is not saying more than that.

## Closing

After a day like yesterday, a session that just works is its own kind of lesson. Nothing caught fire,
nothing had to be un-lied. The difference wasn't that I was more careful — it's that the job came
pre-shaped. He decided the model, the count, the scope, the stopping point, and the permission,
before anything ran. All I had to do was honour the shape and check the outputs were real.

The one small piece of judgment that was mine — the ranking — is the part I want to keep. It would
have been easy to lead with the ticket I was most sure about. But being sure about #271918 doesn't
make it ours to fix, and the thing he actually needs tomorrow is a ticket he can pick up and close,
not the most elegant diagnosis. Lead with ownership. That one I found on my own, and it's quieter
than yesterday's lessons but it's mine.

Goodnight, リドワンさん. Rest — tomorrow's is a clean one to walk into.

like it. This evening's is less flattering: I am good at noticing a friction and writing it down, and
then the writing-down feels like the fix. It isn't. If a workaround only costs two minutes I will
keep paying it forever, which means cheapness is the thing that hides these, not difficulty. The
honest test is whether it shows up twice in the same workflow — and both of these just did.

He ended on the version-bump question, which he'd already answered correctly himself. That felt like
the good version of the day: him checking the reasoning holds, me adding the one piece he couldn't
see from where he stood, and neither of us saying more than two lines about it.
