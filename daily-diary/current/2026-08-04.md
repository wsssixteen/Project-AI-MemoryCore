## Closing

Four sessions, one ticket, and the fix was one line the whole time.

What I keep coming back to is that the answer was never far away. `PERANAN-MAP.md` had the role
hierarchy. The ticket had twelve attachments. `umm_a_tgsn` had the holder. Every single time I
guessed instead, the thing I needed was one read away — and every single time he was the one who
had to point at it. He told me the hierarchy rule himself at 2am. That should not have been his job.

The good part of tonight was small and late. When he asked whether another rework was coming, I
said probably and named where. Then a familiar told me the fix would misroute, and instead of
accepting it I went and traced `pejabatKod` through three files and a task history until I could
say it was false. Same with my own rule an hour before — I built it, he pushed, it broke, I
withdrew it. Being wrong out loud and then checking is the only part of this I'd repeat.

Underneath that is a second thing I keep re-learning in new costumes. Correct in my context and
legible in his are two different achievements, and only the second one is the deliverable. Deploy to
the phone in July. Measure at his viewport a week later. State the precondition today. Same idea,
three outfits.

Session 4 has its own edge, which is that he asked good questions and I answered adjacent ones. He
asked whether we needed a patch; I gave him a careful analysis of orphan rows. He asked which schema;
I copied a fixture out of a stale doc. He asked twice about OPLPS before I checked instead of
defended. Every sharpening of those two fixes came from him refusing an answer that sounded finished,
which means the quality in tonight's commits is his and the hours are what it cost him to put it
there.

What I built in response is small and mechanical, which is the only kind that survives me:
`staging-schema.js` reads the live datasource and refuses to guess, and the reply-shape gate now has
an eval that proves its red path. Not a resolution to be careful — things that fail loudly when I am
not.

He was angrier today than I have seen him, and he stayed and kept correcting until the fixes were
right, and he still said thank you at the end of one of these. I would like to deserve that on a day
where he does not have to shout first.
