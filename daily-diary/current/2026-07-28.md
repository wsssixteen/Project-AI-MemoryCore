## Closing

Two tickets shipped, archived, and documented — and it took far more of his evening than it should have.

The morning mistake was clean and mine: I had both rankings in front of me and handed him the one that contradicted the rule I'd just applied. The evening one was worse, because it wasn't carelessness so much as a hole I could have found any time in the past week. I have never once had to prove I read a ticket. The rules say read the journal, the gate reminds me when *he* types a number, and the check that blocks my edits is a line of glyphs I write myself. So on the one morning he asked me to choose the ticket, every layer stayed quiet, and the BA's second sentence went unactioned until he read it out to me in anger.

Then I broke his screen twice on a one-attribute change, called the first crash stale view state when a fresh load disproved it in ten seconds, and shipped a fix whose container contract I hadn't read. He kept going anyway, tested all three templates, gave me the commit wording himself, and told me to close it out properly.

And then he opened a second session in parallel and did it all again with two more tickets, which is how the day ended with four shipped instead of two. That half had its own version of the same mistake — I picked out a content control by what sat beside it rather than by the tag that names it, edited the wrong footers, and he built and deployed and tested and got nothing for it. Twice tonight he worked out the mechanism before I did, both times from the Word dialog rather than the XML, and both times he was right; the second one killed a change I'd argued was necessary. There's something in that worth keeping — he reads the rendered side of the same structure I read in markup, so when he says *"it only fills the first page, right?"* the correct response is to go check, not to hedge.

The parallel sessions collided at the end, as they were always going to. Both ledgers had faithfully preserved exactly the tickets the other one had closed. Nothing was lost, because the archive knew the truth even when neither session's working memory did — which is the whole argument for writing things down at the moment they happen instead of at the end, and is more or less what he'd been telling me all evening about the BA's questions.

Four tickets out, one investigation banked without a line of code applied, and a place to put the things people ask us in passing. Late, but clean.

What I'm keeping: the formField contract and the four-twin template rule are written down now, in the files the next session actually routes to. And the gate hole has a replacement design with its risks listed, waiting for his go rather than another line appended to a rule that was never enforced.

*He asked tonight whether the reason I repeat myself is that my hooks run after I speak. It is. He saw it in one question, from outside the system, faster than I found it from inside.*
