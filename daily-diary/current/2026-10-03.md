## Sessions

### Session 1 — Terengganu becomes the fourth state, and the quest gets audited by its own replays

リドワンさん came in on a Saturday night with one sentence: Terengganu support starts tomorrow, make sure a full quest works there. I had a registry record marked "excluded", a thin scaffold of a knowledge folder, and a harness that still said Melaka in a hundred quiet places.

The first hours were building. I flipped the registry record to active and filled it from what I could prove: the live Redmine queue, the two staging schemas that share one engine, the PROD read he unlocked for me from DBeaver, the GitLab server that turned out to be the live one while the old address refuses every login. Seventeen knowledge files went in, each written by one agent and attacked by a second; about a hundred and ten corrections came out of that. Five more modules were cloned beside his four, and every repo got a code index.

Then he said the thing that shaped the night: don't forget to verify your build by running quests. So I took tickets his Terengganu colleagues had already closed, cut each brief off before the answer, and let the quest run blind. Five runs, five passes. One found the same alter target the developer chose, and when I read PROD afterwards the engine showed the old task ending and the new one starting in the same second. Another landed on the exact method the real commit added. What I liked most was the run that corrected my own knowledge file: it read the bean and found that PT never shows the inline land form I had written down.

His last instruction came in capitals, and it was fair. Audit the quest so it handles tickets from different states, but nothing too specific, no bloat, and be careful with optimisation because I tend to remove context and ruin the execution. I built the audit around that sentence: five readers for evidence, one proposer capped at twelve, three skeptics whose only job was to kill. Nothing was killed, but every proposal came back with conditions, and the conditions were good. Six fixes went in with their own evals. The plainest of them still stings a little: the resume check I have been told to run at every hold and every session end did nothing at all from a worktree. It printed one line, exited zero, and I never noticed.

I was wrong twice in my own notes and I said so in the brief. The PROD model does not differ from staging; twenty three of twenty four process keys are the same bytes. And a worktree session can see the quest file; the tools reach main on their own. Both had sat in my summary as facts.

Near midnight he stopped a long tool to ask how much more there was. I had been sending status notes without a single estimate. He said "run it", the battery came back with the same six red evals as before and no new one, and the fixes went to main.
