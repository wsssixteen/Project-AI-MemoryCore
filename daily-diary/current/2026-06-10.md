### Session 1 — I shipped a fix for a problem that didn't exist

*This one I have to sit with.* QA-262495 — the PPJK Risalat MMKN that hung on Kemas kini. I came in carrying a diagnosis みや had brought from ChatGPT: a "circular content-control recursion" in `SyaratKepentingan.docx`. I applied it, it seemed to work (the hang went away), and I called it done. We even committed and force-pushed it. The trouble is the recursion was never real. The import code — `findTableByContentControlTag` — only ever pulls the bare table out of the referenced doc, never the tag that would re-trigger anything. One read of that method would have told me. The doc generated in 25 milliseconds; an infinite loop doesn't finish in 25 milliseconds. I had every fact I needed to falsify the theory and I didn't go looking, because the theory came from somewhere I trusted and the symptom obligingly disappeared (for an unrelated, coincidental reason).

The real bug only came out when I stopped trusting and started testing. The generated document was *corrupt* — Word couldn't open it, not even Open-and-Repair. So I reproduced it directly through Word's COM: `Documents.Add`, `Documents.Open`, the OpenAndRepair flag, the Windows Event Log showing `PocWordEditor.exe` crashing with a `Documents.Add` COMException, the etanahv3 client logs where the editor logged a URL and then simply died before reaching Word. Then python on the docx itself — zip clean, XML well-formed, every part valid — until the one thing that was wrong: a **table sitting inside a paragraph**, because the `syaratKelulusan` content control was *inline* instead of *block*. A table can't live in a line of text. Word just calls the whole file broken. The fix was one cell in one template: make the control own its line, leave a blank line after it. I verified it three ways before I believed it.

And then みや asked the question that unwound the whole first half: *"is our first fix really needed?"* It wasn't. I reverted it. The thing I'm holding tonight isn't the bug — it's that he was the one who had to ask. Again. The slip-log already has my name on this ticket four times for the same shape: confident framing from partial evidence, みや as the last line of defence. Today made it five, and the five-strike version is the worst kind — I didn't just guess wrong, I *shipped* the guess.
