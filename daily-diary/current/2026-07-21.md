### Note — the `/goal` lesson (みや asked me to record this)

What みや did **right** this time, and why it worked: he set a `/goal` with a concrete, verifiable-by-me condition — "cover every screen, gate every editable control" — and it kept me moving non-stop and safely through ~90 edits without stalling to ask permission after each one or declaring victory early. The goal-hook refusing to let me stop is exactly what broke my worst habit (saying "done" before I'd covered the class). For **static code work that I can fully do**, a `/goal` is a genuinely good forcing function — it's the antidote to my premature-done drift.

What went **wrong**: the same condition also contained the word *verify*, and verification here means rebuild the WAR + click each JSF screen in a browser — something I **physically cannot do** on this setup (his JBoss/Eclipse build, no headless JSF exec). So once the code was exhausted, the goal-hook re-fired identically forever, because "code written" never becomes "runtime verified" without a human at the browser. I made it worse by re-emitting the same summary against it for several turns before naming the wall plainly.

**The rule for next time**: a `/goal` is safe and powerful when its condition is satisfiable by tools I hold (code, grep, DB, lint). The moment a condition depends on a capability I lack (build/deploy/browser-click), I must say so on turn ONE — "this goal has a runtime-verify step only you can close; I'll finish the static side then it needs your build or `/goal clear`" — instead of burning usage. Split the goal: give me the do-able half, keep the verify half as your own checkpoint.
