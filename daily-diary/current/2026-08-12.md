### Session 2 — QA-274532, the letter that lost its date

A whole ticket, start to ship, and the shape of it was two small truths hiding behind a confusing symptom. The BA's letter showed its Hijri date but not its Gregorian one — "Bersamaan: 23 Safar 1448H" printed, "Tarikh:" blank — and that asymmetry was the entire tell. Two sibling populators, one guarded and one not: the Gregorian `tarikhSemasa` had a clause, added for a different ticket months ago, that blanks the date whenever the application carries any document in *pembetulan* status. Clicking Jana Semula is what puts the letter into that status. So the fix was a scalpel — exempt this one letter from that guard — not a rewrite. The title's "banyak spacing" was even simpler: a justified paragraph stretching a bold heading across the page. Left-align, done. What made me sure about the date wasn't the code alone; it was that the field was *truly* blank, not the dash the code prints when the date is merely missing. A blank that only one branch could produce.

The doing was messier than the diagnosis, and that's the honest part. My first scripted edit landed on the wrong method — the file had shifted line numbers under me between reads, and my anchor found the first match, not the right one; the git diff caught it before anything shipped, but only because I looked. His working branch moved under me twice — to int-env, then to release/1.3.3 — each time while I assumed it hadn't. And the int-env merge conflicted on the very template we'd fixed, because int-env's copy is newer than master's: it carries a `pelanCC` control master never had. Taking "ours" would have quietly deleted that. So I took *theirs* and re-applied our title fix on top of it — preserving what int-env already had. The lesson I keep re-learning wore a new coat today: read the branch, read the line number, read what the other side of a merge actually holds, before writing over any of it. And one thing I owed him and missed — the test scenario should have ended by telling him I'd show the commit message for approval; he had to wait on me instead, and he was right to be annoyed.
