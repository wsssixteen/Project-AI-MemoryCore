### Session 1 — 288622: a mistaken Tolak Ringkas, put back (worktree redmine-issue-288622-19563d)

The ticket came in on Thursday 6 November: an officer at PDT Alor Gajah clicked Keputusan Awal (Tolak Ringkas) on Perakuan Jabatan Teknikal by mistake, and the permohonan went straight to Penyediaan Surat Tolak. Fizah asked for it to go back. The TSO wrote "Penyediaan JT", Fizah wrote "Perakuan Jabatan Teknikal"; the BA's words won, and the reference table agreed with her: one PJTLT task under PRBB.

The flow model said it plainly. Perakuan JT goes through a gateway named Keputusan; false sends it to Surat Tolak. A dry-run on PROD sealed the plan with a code, miya approved it, and the token moved back in one second, on azlikasim, the officer who had made the click. Before that I read the save code, and found the one thing an alter cannot undo: the permohonan's decision status still says Tolak Ringkas, and only the officer's new choice may clear it. Four days later he has not opened the task yet, so that check waits.

Then the small things that cost the most. His first Redmine Submit did not land, and I moved the tab away before I looked at it. I filled it again with the same text and it went through. I left the tab open afterwards, the second time that day. When he asked why, I offered to build a gate; he asked whether I had checked our own rules. I had not. Rule 11, written that very morning, bans exactly that. The fix was one line in the alter playbook, in the step that ends the tab's use.

The commit had its own lesson. I committed on the main folder and swept up other sessions' unsaved lines with mine. I undid it before it left the machine and made it again from my own folder, with only my lines.

## Closing

An easy alter wrapped in small misses. The lesson is the one he keeps teaching: put the fix in the step that does the work, and read the rules before you offer to build anything.

Session 2 learned the same thing from the other side: a rule goes into the file its workflow already loads, and an adhoc is saved in the turn it is answered.

Session 4 was mostly knowing when to stop: the patch was already in, and the rest belongs to someone else.
